## 1. Purpose

Each school will report the competency of its faculty every year through one common framework: five dimensions and 25 indicators, each normalised per faculty member, benchmarked within its discipline and explained in a one-page narrative. The results serve faculty development, school planning and accreditation (NAAC, NIRF and NBA) from a single data set. In the first two years they are **not** linked to individual pay or promotion.

## 2. Design principles

1. **Competence, not credentials.** Measure what faculty do and achieve, not only what they hold.
2. **Ratios, not raw counts.** Every indicator is per faculty member or a percentage, so small and large schools compare fairly.
3. **Quality before quantity.** Count only recognised outlets and roles with evidence of output.
4. **Spread, not stars.** Show how many faculty contribute, not only the total.
5. **Benchmark within the discipline.** Compare each school with its discipline's peers and with its own trend, never in a league table across disciplines.
6. **Numbers plus a narrative.** Every scorecard carries one page explaining what the numbers show and what they miss.
7. **Hard to game.** Evidence rules, random audits and a cap on any single indicator's weight.

## 3. Definitions and rules

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Unit | The school (or college, until schools are formed). |
| Period | Rolling three academic years; trend shown year by year. |
| Faculty count (FTE) | Full-time faculty on the rolls for at least six months of the year; part-time and visiting faculty at 0.5 or less. |
| Normalisation | Per FTE faculty member per year, or a percentage of FTE faculty. |
| Qualifying research outputs | Indexed in Scopus or Web of Science, or on the institution's approved list for the discipline; journals flagged as predatory excluded. Discipline equivalents in Section 5. |
| Evidence | Each claim carries proof (DOI, order, minutes, receipt, certificate). IQAC audits a random 10% each year. |
| Data owner | IQAC compiles; the Dean verifies and writes the narrative; the Academic Council reviews. |
| Accreditation links | NAAC and NIRF references point to the criterion or parameter family; confirm current numbering before the first cycle. |

## 4. The framework: five dimensions, 25 indicators

Default weights total 100. Section 5 adjusts them by discipline. Indicators within a dimension carry equal weight unless the school agrees otherwise.

### Dimension 1. Qualification and development (weight 15)

| # | Indicator | How it is calculated | Links |
|----|----------------------|------------------------------|----------|
| 1.1 | Faculty with a PhD | % of FTE faculty | NIRF FQE |
| 1.2 | Faculty working towards a PhD | % of faculty without a PhD who are registered | NAAC 2 |
| 1.3 | Professional or industry experience | % of faculty with 2 or more years in industry, practice or the field | NIRF FQE |
| 1.4 | Faculty development | Hours per faculty member per year (FDPs, certified courses, industry immersion) | NAAC 6 |

### Dimension 2. Teaching and learning (weight 25)

| # | Indicator | How it is calculated | Links |
|----|----------------------|------------------------------|----------|
| 2.1 | Outcome attainment | % of courses meeting their course-outcome attainment target | NAAC 2.6, NBA |
| 2.2 | Student feedback on teaching | Mean score on a validated instrument, with response rate | NAAC 1.4 |
| 2.3 | Peer-observed teaching | % of faculty observed in the year and rated proficient or above | NAAC 2 |
| 2.4 | Active pedagogy | % of courses using case, project, experiential, field or studio methods | NAAC 2.3 |
| 2.5 | Mentoring | Students per mentor, and % of mentees with a documented plan | NAAC 2.3 |

### Dimension 3. Research quality and impact (weight 25)

| # | Indicator | How it is calculated | Links |
|----|----------------------|------------------------------|----------|
| 3.1 | Research output | Qualifying outputs per faculty member per year | NIRF PU |
| 3.2 | Quality of outlets | % of outputs in the top two quartiles (Q1 and Q2) of the field | NIRF QP |
| 3.3 | Citation impact | Field-weighted citation impact over three years (database value) | NIRF QP |
| 3.4 | Research-active faculty | % of faculty with at least one qualifying output in three years | NAAC 3 |
| 3.5 | Research funding | External grants per faculty member (Rs lakh), and % of faculty who are principal investigators | NAAC 3.1 |
| 3.6 | Intellectual property | Patents, design registrations and plant variety or technology releases, filed and granted, per 10 faculty | NIRF IPR |
| 3.7 | Doctoral supervision | PhD scholars enrolled and completed per PhD-holding faculty member | NAAC 3 |

### Dimension 4. Professional and industry engagement (weight 20)

| # | Indicator | How it is calculated | Links |
|----|----------------------|------------------------------|----------|
| 4.1 | Consultancy and sponsored projects | Revenue received per faculty member per year (Rs lakh) | NIRF FPPP |
| 4.2 | Industry in the classroom | % of courses co-designed or co-taught with practitioners | NAAC 1 |
| 4.3 | Live work for students | Internships, live and field projects generated per 100 students | NAAC 1.3 |
| 4.4 | Professional roles held | Weighted roles per 10 faculty (chair or office-bearer 3; member with output 2; member 1): professional bodies, expert committees, editorial boards, PhD committees and examining, government advisory roles, company boards. | NIRF FPPP |
| 4.5 | Executive and community training | Participant-hours of training delivered to industry, government or farmers | NIRF FPPP |

### Dimension 5. Institutional and societal contribution (weight 15)

| # | Indicator | How it is calculated | Links |
|----|----------------------|------------------------------|----------|
| 5.1 | Curriculum leadership | Board of Studies roles and new courses or programmes designed, per 10 faculty | NAAC 1 |
| 5.2 | Extension and outreach | Activities and people reached (farmers, schools, community) | NAAC 3.6 |
| 5.3 | Institution building | Committee roles with documented outputs, per 10 faculty | NAAC 6 |
| 5.4 | Active collaborations | Agreements with at least one output in the year (joint paper, project, exchange) | NAAC 3.7 |

The five agri-food-bio colleges are placed in their families below. Other schools of the proposed university take the family that fits them.

| Discipline family | Schools | Weights D1 · D2 · D3 · D4 · D5 | What counts as a research output, and other changes |
|------------------|------------------|------------|------------------------------|
| Engineering and technology | Agricultural Engineering and Technology; Food Technology; Engineering; Computer Science | 15 · 25 · 25 · 25 · 10 | Indexed papers; prototypes, patents and testing services. Industry engagement weighted up. |
| Agriculture and life sciences | Agriculture; Biotechnology | 15 · 20 · 25 · 15 · 25 | Indexed papers; technologies, varieties and protocols released; field demonstrations. Extension weighted up; biotech start-ups count under 3.6. |
| Pharmacy | Pharmacy | 15 · 20 · 30 · 20 · 15 | Indexed papers, patents, industry and clinical collaborations. Regulatory norms apply to 1.1. |
| Management and commerce | Agriculture Business Management; Management; Commerce | 15 · 25 · 20 · 25 · 15 | Indexed and ABDC-listed journals, published cases, books. Executive education and consultancy weighted up; use 3.3 with care. |
| Arts, humanities and social sciences | Arts and social sciences | 15 · 30 · 20 · 10 · 25 | Books and chapters from recognised publishers (weighted), translations, policy reports. Citation impact optional. |
| Design, fine and performing arts | Design; Fine Arts; Performing Arts | 15 · 25 · 20 · 25 · 15 | Juried exhibitions, performances, commissions, design registrations, festival selections, judged by portfolio peer review. Practice weighted up. |

**Benchmarks.** Set each family's benchmark from comparable institutions (for example, top-band NIRF peers in the discipline and similar private universities). In year one, use each school's own baseline.

## 6. Scoring

**Step 1. Score each indicator** against its benchmark (invert for indicators where lower is better, such as students per mentor):

| Level | Value as % of benchmark | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Emerging | below 60% | Well below discipline peers |
| 2 · Developing | 60 to 89% | Approaching peers |
| 3 · Proficient | 90 to 119% | At peer level |
| 4 · Leading | 120% or above | Among the best peers |

**Steps 2 to 4.** Dimension score = average level of its indicators (1.0 to 4.0). School profile = the five dimension scores plus an overall score weighted by the family's weights; no indicator may carry more than 20% of the total. Trend = improving, steady or declining over three years.

**Worked example (illustrative figures).** A school has 30 FTE faculty.

| Indicator | School value | Benchmark | % of benchmark | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Faculty with a PhD | 18 of 30 = 60% | 70% | 86% | 2 · Developing |
| 3.1 Research output | 42 outputs ÷ (30 × 3 years) = 0.47 | 0.60 | 78% | 2 · Developing |

## 9. Safeguards against gaming

- Count outputs only in recognised outlets; exclude predatory journals and duplicate publication.
- Count roles only with evidence of output (minutes, reports, examiner letters), and revenue only when received.
- IQAC audits a random 10% of claims each year; unsupported claims are removed and noted.
- Report spread (3.4, and the share of roles held by the top five faculty) alongside totals.
- Read numbers with the narrative and the three-year trend, never alone.
- Keep the framework developmental for the first two years: no link to individual pay or promotion.
