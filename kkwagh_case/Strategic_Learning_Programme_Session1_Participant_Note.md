**About the programme**

The Strategic Learning Programme is a series of fortnightly 90-minute sessions for the senior leaders of the K K Wagh Education Society. Each session uses a short case or a live issue to look at how we lead: how we decide what our work is for, how we decide together, and how we keep improving what we already do well. The sessions are discussions among peers, not lectures. One session will not change how any of us works, so each one builds on the last and on what we try in between.

**About the case**

*The Demo That Passed* is about a successful, mid-sized company whose Board asks it to develop AI-driven products. The leadership team is capable, busy and confident it already has what is needed. Then new information arrives, and the Managing Director must decide what to do by Monday. No technical knowledge is needed. The case is set in a company, not an educational institution, on purpose: it lets us look at familiar leadership questions from a little distance before we bring them home.

**Objectives of the session**

1. To examine how a capable team decides what a task is really for, and how it knows whether its work is succeeding.
2. To explore when a leader should hold firm, when to accommodate a colleague, and when to step beyond their own role.
3. To compare what we say good leadership looks like with the choices we actually make under pressure.
4. To leave with one or two practices to try in our own areas before the next session.

**Preparation (about 15 minutes)**

- Please do not look for the case in advance. It is handed out in three short parts during the session, and reading it fresh is part of the design.
- Note down your answers to two questions. They are for you, not for submission.
    1. Name one thing in your area that is "working well". How do you know? Whose view, outside your own team, have you asked?
    2. Recall a recent disagreement with a colleague that was settled politely. Was it really settled?
- Please arrive five minutes early. The session opens on time with a short, silent exercise.

**Be prepared to**

- write down your own view before you hear anyone else's;
- choose between options when every option has a real cost, and predict what a team will actually do;
- argue, for part of the discussion, from the viewpoint of a character whose role is unlike your own;
- disagree openly and courteously, and change your mind when the argument is better;
- relate the discussion to your own institution and your own area at the end.

**Group work**

- **Tables.** You will sit in mixed groups of four to six, drawn from different institutions and functions, and not with colleagues in your own reporting line. Groups stay the same for the first three sessions, so that trust and candour can build.
- **In the session.** Individual writing, pair discussion, table discussion, a short role play in character, and an anonymous room poll. Each table names a reporter; the role rotates each session.
- **Between sessions.** You will be paired with a learning partner from another institution. Before session 2, hold one 15-minute conversation on how your two commitments are going.
- **For session 2.** Bring one current initiative from your own area. We will ask two questions of it: what is it for, and how would you improve it?

**Ground rules**

The case is fictional, and no one's institution, department or performance is being reviewed. Argue positions, not people. Seniority does not decide the answer. What individuals say stays in the room: ideas may travel, names do not. Phones on silent, unless you are using the interactive case, which runs offline and stores nothing.
