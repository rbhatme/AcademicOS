# K K Wagh Proposed University: Infra and Financial Plan, Critical Review

Inputs: Design Brief (19 Sep 2026), Financial Calculation PDF, Admin and Optimum Academic Area workbook (3 Oct 2026).
Figures in Rs Cr unless stated. "Recalc" = my recalculation from the plan's own numbers. Regulatory points are flagged "verify" where I have not checked the current rule.

## 1. Verdict
The financial plan, as it stands, does not show a viable project. On its own numbers the project loses money for five years, and debt service coverage (DSCR) is 0.05 to 0.76 in years 1 to 5. The 10-year cash table that looks healthy (cumulative Rs 207 Cr) is an artefact of broken formulas. The plan funds roughly one-sixth of what the design brief describes, and several core items are missing. It cannot yet go to a lender, the Board or a regulator.

## 2. Plan vs brief: the scale gap (most important)

| Item | Design brief | Financial / area plan | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Students | 30,000 | 4,464 (4,134 in area sheet) | 15% |
| Schools | 20 (12 buildings) | 3 | 17 missing |
| Built area | about 47.6 lakh sq ft | about 2.9 to 3.3 lakh sq ft (area workbook, incl. circulation) | about 7% |
| Academic area | 30 lakh sq ft (100 sq ft/student) | 14,421 sqm = 1.55 lakh sq ft, about 38 sq ft/student net | about 5% |
| Hostels | 1,600 beds, 5 lakh sq ft | none | missing |
| CBSE school, faculty housing, guest house, visiting-faculty hostel, student centre, medical, transport, utilities | listed | none | missing |
| Project cost | not stated | Rs 160 Cr | see section 3 |

- The plan is a Phase-1 plan but is never labelled as one. There is no phasing path from 4,464 to 30,000 students, no capex or funding for later phases, and no statement of when the 88.5 acres are built out.
- The Rs 160 Cr cost cannot cover the brief. At Rs 3,000 per sq ft (my assumption), 47.6 lakh sq ft is about Rs 1,400 Cr. Even the Phase-1 area (about 3 lakh sq ft) at Rs 2,500 to 3,000 is Rs 75 to 100 Cr, against civil cost of Rs 32.75 Cr in the plan (about Rs 1,000 per sq ft, recalc). Confirm the construction rate with a quantity surveyor.
- Academic area per student (38 sq ft net, about 52 sq ft with 40% circulation) is about half the brief's 100 sq ft. The workbook is named "Optimum". If compression is deliberate, state it and check it against AICTE and NBA norms. If not, the brief and the plan contradict each other.

## 3. Financial plan: errors and weaknesses

### 3.1 Results as presented
- Profit before depreciation: -10.5, -5.1, +1.8, +4.3, +5.6 (years 1 to 5). After depreciation, losses run through year 5.
- DSCR: 0.05, 0.54, 0.61, 0.71, 0.76. The usual bank threshold is about 1.25 (verify with the lender).
- The plan itself says break-even is above 100% of capacity until year 4.

### 3.2 Years 6 to 10 are wrong
- Fee collection shows #REF! and "1,578". Intake shows 90. "Plant occupancy" shows "1005".
- Salary and wages in years 6 to 10 drop to "-", "1", "3,50,000". The cash table therefore carries no salary cost. Average DSCR is #REF!.
- Recalc with salary held at the year-5 level of Rs 30.7 Cr, same fees and costs:
  - Profit before depreciation, years 6 to 10: 6.7, 7.8, 8.9, 10.0, 11.1.
  - Cumulative over 10 years: about +40 Cr, against Rs 80 Cr of principal due in years 3 to 10.
  - Cumulative cash after principal repayment: about **-Rs 40 Cr** at year 10 (the plan shows +207 Cr).
  - DSCR years 6 to 10: 0.81, 0.86, 0.93, 1.00, 1.08.
- So the loan cannot be repaid from operations within the 10 years shown, even before the issues below.

### 3.3 Arithmetic and consistency errors
- **Interest rate:** stated as 15% p.a. (section 6), computed at 11% (Rs 11 Cr on Rs 100 Cr), and noted as 10% (section 3.8). At 15% the year-1 interest is Rs 15 Cr.
- **Means of finance:** text says 15% equity and 85% loan. Numbers are Rs 60 Cr equity (37.5%) and Rs 100 Cr loan (62.5%).
- **Project cost:** Rs 160 Cr, but civil Rs 32.75 Cr plus plant and machinery Rs 78 Cr is Rs 110.75 Cr. Rs 49.25 Cr is unexplained. It may be land, furniture, IT, books, pre-operative cost or contingency. Nothing says so.
- **Stale labels:** the depreciation lines say "187.5 Lacs civil" and "950 Lacs plant", against Rs 32.75 Cr and Rs 78 Cr.
- **Year-1 R&M:** Rs 95.4 lakh in section 3.7, Rs 4.3 lakh in the profitability table. Year-1 cost is understated by about Rs 0.9 Cr.
- **Lab expense, year 2:** uses 3,344 students instead of 2,964. Overstated by about Rs 0.3 Cr.
- **Staff expense, year 4:** uses 5,044 students instead of 4,464.
- **Wrong cell references:** the gross margin is labelled "(3.1 - 3.2.6)" and the profit before depreciation "(2.3-2.6)". These point to the wrong lines.
- **Fee line:** year 10 fee is 1,578 (a unit slip).
- **B.Tech in year 1:** 960 students against an intake of 480, then 1,440 in year 2. This implies about 480 existing students move over from day one. If that is true, say so (and the existing college's staff, assets and liabilities are missing from the plan). If it is an error, fee revenue is overstated by Rs 9.6 Cr in each of years 1 to 3.
- **Pharmacy and B.Design:** appear in fee and salary tables with zero students. These look like leftover rows.

### 3.4 Revenue assumptions
- 100% of intake filled and 100% retained, from year 1. The header row mentions 80% and 90% occupancy, but the calculations use 100%.
- No fee escalation, no scholarships, discounts, attrition, bad debts or fee-regulation effects. (Verify fee-setting freedom under the state self-financed universities law.)
- BA (Liberal, Performing, Fine Arts) fee is Rs 30,000 against per-student variable cost of about Rs 38,650. Each BA student loses money before salary.
- BCS, BBA, BCA at Rs 1 lakh sit 8 to 10x below the programme cost and the "world-class" positioning. B.Tech (Rs 2 lakh) is 63% of revenue.
- No other income: hostel and mess, CBSE school, transport, exam and other fees, research and consultancy, executive education, incubator, auditorium and facility rental, canteen concession, CSR, alumni.

### 3.5 Cost assumptions
- All variable costs are "per student, ref: Engineering". The same Rs 38,650 per student is applied to arts, finance and management. No programme-wise costing.
- No faculty for **BCS** (480 students). Recalc: about 24 faculty, about Rs 1.8 Cr a year, missing from the salary table.
- No cost for PhD supervision (24 scholars), library books and e-resources, accreditation (NAAC, NBA, NIRF, AACSB-type), faculty development, research seed grants, scholarships, insurance, ERP licences and cloud, or pre-launch costs.
- Salaries are flat for 10 years: no increments, no PF, gratuity, ESI or medical (about 12 to 15% on-cost), no inflation on any cost.
- Pay scales (Assistant Professor Rs 50,000 to 75,000; Professor Rs 2 lakh) are at the low end for a university that wants to be "world-class". Check against UGC and AICTE norms and your hiring market.
- Student-faculty ratio stated as 20:1, but the plan has 198 faculty for 4,464 students (22.5:1). The Dean count is 5, while the brief has 20 schools and the area sheet has 6 to 10 Dean offices.
- Admin and supporting staff (62 + 122) conflicts with the area sheet (the admissions cell alone is planned for 50 people; the plan has about 45 marketing and executive staff in total). There are no hostel, mess, warden, hospital or sports staff.
- Power Rs 2,000 per student is about Rs 0.9 Cr a year. An AC-heavy campus of about 3 lakh sq ft is likely above that. Marketing is flat Rs 5 Cr (17% of year-1 fees).

### 3.6 What is missing from the capital plan
- Land: 88.5 acres, not costed, and no statement of ownership, transfer, valuation or conversion.
- Working capital. Year-1 and year-2 losses (about Rs 10.5 Cr + Rs 5.1 Cr before principal) are not funded within the Rs 160 Cr.
- Contingency, escalation, GST on construction, pre-operative and approval costs, interest during construction.
- Campus utilities and external works: substation, DG sets, STP, water and fire tanks, roads, parking, landscape, security systems, renewable energy. All listed in the brief.
- Hostels, transport fleet (vehicle capex is zero; only Rs 250 per student a year for running), sports fields, medical.
- Statutory requirements for a state private university (corpus or endowment, security deposit, minimum land and facilities, timelines). Verify under the state Act and UGC and AICTE rules.
- Phasing: no construction schedule, no link between first-year occupancy and the date buildings are ready.

## 4. Infrastructure and area workbook: errors and gaps

### 4.1 Workbook integrity
- #VALUE! errors in the lab-size and project-room totals (R15, AF15, R37, AF37). The cause is text like "20-25" in numeric cells.
- Three or more different academic areas: 14,421 (summary), 13,986 (Phase-1), 13,897 (calc), 15,000 ("say").
- The "to be considered" column totals 10,832 sqm, not 14,421. It omits seminar halls, computer centres, HoD offices, meeting rooms, department libraries and project labs (about 2,700 sqm). The 14,421 total comes from a different column. The two do not reconcile.
- Admin area totals do not match: 12,670 (sum), 12,278 and 8,770 (derived), 6,625 (hard-coded "All"). Several figures are typed in, not formulas.
- Circulation is applied at 33%, 40% and 50% in different places. Pick one and justify it.
- Enrolment in the workbook (4,134) differs from the financial plan (4,464):
  - B.Tech CSE 180 here vs 240 there; Mechanical 120 here but absent in the plan; MCA 0 here vs 120 there; B.Sc Finance 120 here vs 60 there.
  - The totals match at 1,254 intake only by coincidence.

### 4.2 Content gaps
- **No hostels.** For a first-generation, rural-first student base and a 88.5-acre campus, hostel is a core capacity driver and a major revenue line. Brief says 1,600 beds; plan has none.
- **Library:** 2,000 sqm full / 1,500 sqm Phase-1 (about 16,000 sq ft) vs 1.5 lakh sq ft in the brief. Seating (500) is about 11% of students, fine for Phase-1.
- **Performing Arts and Fine Arts:** about 316 sqm each for 90 students per school. No studios, rehearsal rooms, black box, exhibition or workshop space. Underspecified for the discipline.
- **Engineering labs:** a uniform 75 sqm lab regardless of discipline. Mechanical and other heavy-equipment labs, workshops and foundry-type spaces need more. Check against the AICTE norm by programme.
- **Classrooms:** 36 rooms for 4,134 students (about 2,500 seats). This assumes strong timetable sharing and no lecture-hall complex, which the brief suggests.
- **Phase-1 auditorium and gym:** zero area in Phase-1. Student centre, bank, sports and helpdesk are also zero. Confirm this matches the admission year.
- **Not in any area sheet:** outdoor sports, parking, STP, power, water, fire, transport, security, CBSE school, residences, research parks, incubator beyond the T&P block, Centres of Excellence, any agri-food-bio-engineering facility.
- **Accessibility, fire, MEP and structural allowances** are only mentioned in the brief. No area or cost.

### 4.3 Design brief
- Objective statements ("world-class", "academic excellence") are generic. No programme or student-experience targets behind them.
- "Budget sensitive" is stated, but no budget, cost per sq ft or phase budget is given to the designers.
- Open items: "Printing press ?" and "Any other facility", plus a struck-out "student union" that should be a decision, not a deletion.
- Vastu, temple and iconic admin building add cost; no allowance or priority rule.
- A CBSE school (2,000 students, 2.5 lakh sq ft) is in the brief but in neither the financials nor the area workbook. It could be a separate entity; say so.

## 5. Strategic gaps across all three documents
1. **Identity vs plan.** The vision workshop and the school skill-list work centre on agri-food-bio-engineering-business, the Society's strength. The financial plan has only Technology, Computer/Commerce/Management and Arts. Agriculture, Food Technology, Biotechnology and Agri-Engineering are absent. Pharmacy and B.Design appear as zero rows. Decide whether those colleges (currently affiliated to another university) fold in, partner, or stay out, and cost the choice.
2. **Why this mix?** No demand evidence (Nashik and Maharashtra seat vacancy by programme, competitor fees, employer demand) for the 9 programmes or for a 30,000 target.
3. **Programme economics.** 63% of revenue from one programme (B.Tech), at risk from AICTE approval limits, vacancy in engineering, and fee rules.
4. **Quality and regulation.** No accreditation roadmap (NAAC, NBA, NIRF, international). No approvals timeline (state Act, UGC, AICTE, others). No academic governance cost.
5. **Skill-first promise.** The vision is skill and practice-led, but there is no budget for labs, workshops, industry co-design, certification or internships beyond Rs 8,000 per student a year.
6. **Talent.** No faculty hiring plan, research culture, PhD pipeline or incentive model.
7. **Risk and governance.** No scenario analysis, no contingency, no related-party treatment between the Society, the existing colleges and the university (shared staff, land, brand fees).
8. **Technology.** Smart campus is stated; no ERP, LMS, data, cyber-security or AI budget.
9. **Sustainability.** Net-zero and renewable are goals with no capex or savings model.
10. **Exit and funding.** Loan terms (15%?), moratorium, security, covenants and the equity source are unspecified. As a trust, regulatory limits on profit and surplus use apply (verify).

## 6. Priority fixes (in order)
1. Decide Phase-1 scope in writing: student numbers, programmes, buildings, opening date. Show the road to 30,000 as phases with capex and funding.
2. Rebuild the financial model in one workbook with linked formulas: no #REF!, ten-year horizon, programme-wise revenue and cost, increments, on-costs, escalation, scholarships, 90 to 95% fill, retention.
3. Reconcile project cost line by line (land, civil by area at a QS-verified rate, equipment, IT, library, furniture, utilities, hostel, pre-operative, contingency, working capital, interest during construction).
4. Fix means of finance: one loan rate, one equity share, a repayment schedule that the cash flow can carry (DSCR of 1.25 or more), or raise equity, add phasing, or add revenue (hostel, mess, executive education).
5. Reconcile enrolment and programme list across the three documents (B.Tech split, MCA, B.Sc Finance, BCS faculty, existing B.Tech cohort in year 1).
6. Reconcile area sheet totals and circulation percentage; remove #VALUE! errors; link summary cells to detail.
7. Add hostels, discipline-specific lab and studio space, and utilities to the area programme; cost them.
8. Test viability under three cases (base, 90% fill, 80% fill with 15% interest) and show the break-even enrolment and fee level.
9. Align programmes with the Society's agri-food-bio identity, or state why not.
10. Add a regulatory and approvals checklist with dates and costs.

## 7. Limits of this review

- Cost per sq ft, DSCR threshold and salary comparisons are my assumptions and should be confirmed with a quantity surveyor, lender and HR benchmark.
- Statutory and AICTE/UGC points are flagged "verify" and were not checked against current notifications.
