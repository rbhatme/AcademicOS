Topic and mini casePedagogy: teaching that sticks

Mini case: The Tuesday Batch — twenty-two years of perfect notes

Pre-readingNone — the case arrives cold

The vignette is read in silence, on paper, in the first eight minutes

Purpose

This session does not lecture about pedagogy. It runs a mini case about a classroom — cold — and lets the method be the message.

Faculty first sit inside the case as learners, then step outside it as teachers to examine why each move worked: the cold read, the critical questions, the synthesis, the formative check. The same lens is then turned on artificial intelligence: how to keep the productive struggle when AI is in the room, and how to use it as a thinking partner rather than an answer engine.

Core idea: define the problem first

  1. Meet the problemTwenty-two years of perfect notes, and scores flat for a decade
  2. Work it outWho is doing the cognitive work in the 55 minutes?
  3. Discover the conceptsTools the dilemma demands
  4. SynthesiseName what was learnt — and what to show for it

Savita's notes were perfect — and that was the problem.

Sixty-five students, a decade of test scores that never moved, and a feedback form that praises remembering "till the exam". When the NAAC circular asks what the student does in her class, the dilemma cannot be ducked: deliver the chapter for the twenty-second time, or redesign one class and produce evidence. Faculty work the dilemma cold; the concepts — Bloom's verbs, backward design, alignment, engagement at scale — arrive as the tools the problem demands, not as definitions to memorise. Because they build the answer themselves, it sticks.

Behind the dilemma sits a wider choice of pedagogies: ten families compared side by side in a Pedagogy Atlas, each with what it buys and what it costs. And one question that none of them can now avoid: when AI can write the answer, what exactly are we still assessing?

Suggested pacing, to be adjusted to the room.

Learning outcomes by the end of the session

Method

Frame a classroom situation as a decision-forcing mini case, sequence critical questions that surface the concepts, and close with a synthesis that names them.

Content

Explain what learner-centric teaching actually requires — who performs the verbs, how evidence is designed backward from outcomes, and why engagement must scale to 65. Compare ten pedagogical families by what each buys and costs, and describe how to preserve cognitive friction when students use AI.

Bloom's taxonomyBackward designAlignmentOne-minute paperPedagogy AtlasAI-free then AI-mediated
Practice

Redesign ten minutes of your own next class around one higher-order verb, state whether AI is prohibited, permitted, encouraged or required in it, defend the design to a partner, and take a fourteen-item formative MCQ self-check — the same instruments you carry back to your department.

Takeaway

Each faculty member leaves with ten redesigned minutes of their own next class — and the evidence it must produce by Friday.
